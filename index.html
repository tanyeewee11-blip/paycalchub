How Your Take-Home Pay Is Calculated (2026 Tax Rules)

Your net take-home pay is the amount deposited into your bank account after all mandatory payroll taxes, statutory withholdings, and voluntary deductions are subtracted from your gross earnings. While gross pay represents your agreed-upon wage or base salary, statutory deductions can reduce this figure by 15% to over 38% depending on your filing status, tax bracket, and state residency.

The Core Net Pay Equation:

Net Pay = Gross Pay - (Federal Income Tax + State & Local Income Taxes + FICA Taxes + Pre-Tax Deductions)

Mandatory Payroll Withholdings Explained

Every legal employee in the United States is subject to standardized payroll withholdings under federal and local legislation. Here is how your paycheck deductions are allocated:

Deduction Component Statutory Rate Applicable Ceiling / Notes
Federal Income Tax (FIT) Progressive (10% to 37%) Calculated using IRS Form W-4 and Publication 15-T brackets after applying standard deductions.
Social Security (OASDI) 6.20% (Employee portion) Subject to the annual Social Security wage base cap; income above this threshold is exempt.
Medicare (Hospital Insurance) 1.45% (Base rate) No wage limit. An additional 0.9% surtax applies to wages exceeding $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (married).
State & Local Income Tax (SIT) 0.00% to 13.30% 9 states impose zero individual income tax (e.g., TX, FL, WA, NV, TN). Others use graduated or flat scales.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Why does my paycheck seem lower than my calculated salary?

In addition to mandatory federal and state income taxes, employers often subtract non-statutory benefits before depositing funds. Common deductions include employer-sponsored health insurance premiums, dental/vision plans, 401(k) retirement contributions, and Health Savings Account (HSA) allocations.

What is the difference between a tax deduction and a tax credit?

A tax deduction lowers your overall taxable income before tax brackets are applied (e.g., standard deduction, 401k pre-tax contributions). A tax credit, on the other hand, reduces your total tax liability dollar-for-dollar after taxes have been calculated (e.g., Child Tax Credit).

How does moving to a no-income-tax state affect take-home pay?

Relocating from a state with high income taxes (like California or New York) to a state with zero personal income tax (such as Texas or Florida) can increase your net annual take-home pay by 4% to 10% on the same gross salary. However, property taxes and local sales taxes often compensate for the lack of state income taxes.
