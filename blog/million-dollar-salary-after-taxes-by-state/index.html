The headline number: In the nine no-income-tax states, a single filer earning $1,000,000 keeps about $602,030 after federal tax and FICA. In California, the same salary nets about $509,030 — a $93,000/year gap purely from state income tax.

Where Does the Other Half Go?

For a no-tax state, the breakdown on $1,000,000 looks roughly like this: about $324,000 to federal income tax, and about $74,000 to FICA (Social Security up to the wage base, plus 1.45% Medicare, plus the 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax that applies above $200,000 for single filers). State tax then takes another bite depending on where you live — from $0 to over $93,000/year.

Why This Number Gets Misunderstood

A common misconception is that someone earning $1,000,000 pays close to the top 37% rate on the entire amount. In reality, only the portion of income above roughly $640,600 (single filer, 2026) is taxed at 37% — everything below that is taxed at the lower brackets first. The blended (effective) rate on $1,000,000 works out closer to 32-33% federal, not 37%.

⚠️ This uses simplified flat-equivalent state rates for comparison and a single filer with the standard deduction — no itemized deductions, stock compensation timing, or business income treatment, all of which commonly apply at this income level. Frequently Asked Questions

How much do you actually keep from a $1 million salary? +
A single filer earning $1,000,000/year keeps roughly $509,000 to $602,000 after federal income tax and FICA, depending on the state's income tax rate — a gap of about $93,000/year between no-tax states and California.
What tax bracket does $1 million fall into? +
Income above roughly $640,600 (2026, single filer) is taxed at the top 37% federal bracket, but only that portion — not the entire $1 million. The blended effective federal rate on a $1,000,000 salary is closer to 32-33%.
Does a $1 million salary pay extra Medicare tax? +
Yes. In addition to the standard 1.45% Medicare tax, an Additional Medicare Tax of 0.9% applies to wages above $200,000 for single filers ($250,000 married filing jointly).
