$30 an Hour is How Much a Year? (2026 Take-Home Pay Breakdown)

Published by PayCalcHub Research Team • Updated for 2026 Statutory Tax Brackets • 7 min read

Quick Takeaways at $30/Hour

  • Gross Annual Salary: $62,400 (based on 2,080 standard annual work hours).
  • Monthly Gross Pay: $5,200 per month before taxes.
  • Bi-Weekly Paycheck: $2,400 gross every two weeks.
  • Estimated Net Take-Home (Zero-Tax State): Approx. $51,600 to $53,400 / year ($2,060 bi-weekly).
  • Estimated Net Take-Home (California/NY): Approx. $48,200 to $49,500 / year ($1,850 to $1,900 bi-weekly).

1. Gross Pay Breakdown: Daily, Weekly, Bi-Weekly, and Monthly

In the United States, full-time employment is conventionally modeled on an 8-hour workday, 40-hour workweek, and 52-week year. This totals 2,080 paid hours per calendar year. Here is the mathematical conversion across common payroll intervals:

Pay Period Working Hours Gross Earnings ($)
Hourly Rate 1 hour $30.00
Daily Rate 8 hours $240.00
Weekly Pay 40 hours $1,200.00
Bi-Weekly Pay (26 paychecks) 80 hours $2,400.00
Monthly Pay (12 paychecks) ~173.33 hours $5,200.00
Annual Gross Salary 2,080 hours $62,400.00

Note on Paid Time Off: If your employer does not offer paid holidays or paid sick leave, your working hours will realistically fall around 1,920 to 2,000 hours per year, reducing annual gross compensation to approximately $57,600 – $60,000.

2. Statutory Taxes Subtracted from $62,400

Your actual paycheck will reflect three primary statutory withholdings mandated by federal and state governments:

  1. Federal Income Tax (FIT): Under 2026 IRS withholding brackets, a single taxpayer claiming the standard deduction will have taxable income of approximately $47,400 ($62,400 gross minus ~$15,000 standard deduction). Marginal rates span 10% on the first tier and 12% on remaining taxable income, resulting in an estimated federal liability of ~$5,800 to $6,100.
  2. FICA Taxes (7.65%): Mandated by federal law, this consists of 6.20% for Social Security ($3,868.80) and 1.45% for Medicare ($904.80), totaling $4,773.60 per year.
  3. State Income Tax: Depending entirely on where you reside and work, state withholding ranges from 0% (in 9 states) to over 5% in moderate-to-high income tax states.

4. Practical Budget Blueprint on a $30/Hour Wage

Assuming an average monthly take-home pay of $4,250 (after taxes), applying the widely recognized 50/30/20 budgeting rule yields a balanced financial plan:

50% Needs ($2,125 / month)

Covers rent or mortgage, groceries, utilities, vehicle payments/insurance, and minimum debt payments.

30% Wants ($1,275 / month)

Allocated for dining out, streaming subscriptions, hobbies, travel, and personal leisure.

20% Savings & Debt Acceleration ($850 / month)

Dedicated to an emergency cash reserve (3-6 months), Roth IRA retirement contributions, or extra debt principal payoff.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much overtime increases a $30/hr salary?

Under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), overtime is paid at 1.5 times your regular rate (time-and-a-half). At $30/hour, your overtime rate is $45.00 per hour. Working just 5 hours of overtime per week adds $11,700 in gross annual pay, bringing your total income to $74,100.

Is $30 an hour middle class?

Yes. According to the Pew Research Center, middle-class income for a single individual spans roughly two-thirds to double the median national income. Earning $62,400 firmly places a single worker in the solid middle class across most American regions.
