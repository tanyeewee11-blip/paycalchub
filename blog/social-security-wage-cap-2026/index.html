Social Security Wage Cap 2026: The $184,500 Limit Explained

FICA & Social Security June 2026 6 min read

In 2026, Social Security tax stops being withheld once your wages hit $184,500 for the year. Every dollar you earn above that cap costs you 6.2% less in payroll tax — your paycheck actually gets bigger automatically, with no action required on your part.

How the Social Security Wage Cap Works

The Social Security tax rate is 6.2% for employees (your employer pays a matching 6.2%). But unlike Medicare, Social Security has an annual wage ceiling called the wage base limit. For 2026, that limit is $184,500. Once your cumulative W-2 wages for the calendar year exceed this amount, your employer stops withholding Social Security tax from your paycheck for the remainder of the year.

When Does the Cap Hit — By Salary Level

The exact paycheck where Social Security withholding stops depends on how often you are paid:

In the paycheck where you cross the cap, withholding adjusts automatically — you only pay SS tax on the portion up to $184,500, not the full paycheck amount. From that paycheck forward, your take-home pay increases by 6.2% of each gross paycheck for the rest of the year.

Medicare Has No Cap — and Above $200k Gets More Expensive

Medicare works differently. The 1.45% Medicare tax has no wage base limit — it applies to every dollar you earn, no matter how high your income. Above $200,000 in wages (for single filers), an Additional Medicare Tax of 0.9% kicks in, bringing the total Medicare rate to 2.35% on earnings above that threshold. Your employer is required to withhold this extra 0.9% once your wages pass $200,000 in a calendar year, but only the employer’s standard 1.45% continues — the employer does not match the additional 0.9%.

Practical impact: If you earn $250,000/year, you save about $4,030 in SS tax compared to someone earning $184,500 (because of the cap), but you owe an extra $450 in Additional Medicare Tax (0.9% on the $50,000 above $200k). The SS cap benefit still wins, but the Medicare surtax partially offsets it at higher incomes.
⚠️ If you work multiple jobs and your combined W-2 wages from all employers exceed $184,500, each employer withholds SS tax independently up to $184,500 per employer. You may end up overpaying SS during the year — and can claim the excess as a credit on your federal tax return (Form 1040, Schedule 3).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Social Security wage cap for 2026? +
The 2026 Social Security wage base is $184,500. You pay the 6.2% employee Social Security tax only on wages up to this amount. Once your cumulative wages for the year exceed $184,500, Social Security withholding stops for the rest of that calendar year.
What happens to my paycheck when I hit the Social Security wage cap? +
Once your wages reach $184,500 for the year, your employer stops withholding the 6.2% Social Security tax from your paycheck. This means each subsequent paycheck is larger by 6.2% of your gross pay — about $62 more per $1,000 earned above the cap.
Does Medicare have a wage cap? +
No. The 1.45% Medicare tax applies to all wages with no cap. Above $200,000 in wages, an additional 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax applies (for single filers), making the total Medicare rate 2.35% on income above that threshold.
What if I have two jobs and pay too much Social Security tax? +
If multiple employers each withhold SS tax independently and your combined wages exceed $184,500, you’ve overpaid. The excess can be claimed as a credit on your federal return (Form 1040, Schedule 3). Your employer cannot coordinate this across employers — it’s corrected at filing.
