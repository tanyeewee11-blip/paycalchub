Net Pay vs. Gross Pay: What's the Difference?

Gross pay is your salary before anything is taken out. Net pay — also called take-home pay — is what's left after federal income tax, state income tax (if your state has one), Social Security (6.2%), and Medicare (1.45%) are withheld. Net pay is the amount that actually lands in your bank account.

Weekly, Biweekly, Semi-Monthly, or Monthly — Why the Numbers Differ

Your annual net pay is fixed, but how it's split into paychecks depends on your employer's pay schedule:

  • Weekly — 52 paychecks/year. Common in hourly and trade jobs.
  • Bi-weekly — 26 paychecks/year (every other week). The most common pay schedule in the US.
  • Semi-monthly — 24 paychecks/year, typically on fixed dates like the 15th and last day of the month.
  • Monthly — 12 paychecks/year. Less common in the US; more typical for salaried roles at smaller employers.

Biweekly and semi-monthly are the two that trip people up: biweekly gives you 2 extra paychecks per year compared to semi-monthly, which is why two months a year have a "3-paycheck" bi-weekly month, but each individual biweekly check is smaller than a semi-monthly one.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is net pay? +
Net pay is the amount left in your paycheck after federal income tax, state income tax, and FICA (Social Security and Medicare) are withheld from your gross pay. It's also called take-home pay — the amount that actually hits your bank account.
Why is my biweekly net pay not exactly half of semi-monthly? +
Biweekly pay (every 2 weeks) results in 26 paychecks per year, while semi-monthly pay (twice a month) results in 24 paychecks per year. Since your annual salary is the same either way, each biweekly paycheck is slightly smaller than half a semi-monthly paycheck — but you receive 2 more of them per year.
How many paychecks will I get in a year? +
Weekly pay: 52 paychecks/year. Biweekly pay (every 2 weeks): 26 paychecks/year. Semi-monthly pay (twice a month, e.g. 15th and last day): 24 paychecks/year. Monthly pay: 12 paychecks/year.
Is net pay the same as take-home pay? +
Yes, net pay and take-home pay refer to the same thing: your earnings after all mandatory tax withholdings (federal, state, Social Security, Medicare) are subtracted from your gross pay.
Does this include 401(k) or health insurance deductions? +
Yes — use the optional Pre-Tax Deductions section to add your 401(k) and health insurance contributions. These reduce your taxable income before federal and state tax are calculated, which increases your net pay slightly compared to not contributing at all (since less income is taxed), even though the contribution itself reduces your take-home cash.
